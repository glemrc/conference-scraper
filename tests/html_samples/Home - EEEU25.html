Topics

Conference Theme

Entrepreneurship and Human-Centric Business Strategies for Social and Economic Resilience

The scientific committee welcomes submissions for paper in the area of entrepreneurship and economy in the world today. More particularly, but not exclusively, the following tracks about the conference’s overarching theme are listed:

Track 1: Social Problems of Entrepreneurship

A major component in an enterprise’s operation is the human aspects within the organization and the business environment. Consequently, research pertaining to the social dimensions of management and the various levels and domains of individual functioning within an organization is of paramount importance. The psychosocial approach analyzes how personal traits and affiliation with social groups influence individual results. The organizational domain encompasses a critical field of inquiry: human resources management, which includes the features of functions, key regions of influence, and ethical considerations in corporate operations. Currently, the social sector of entrepreneurship is increasingly focusing on issues such as the employment of immigrants, diversity, equity, inclusion, anti-racism, social entrepreneurship, and academic entrepreneurship.

Track 2: Entrepreneurship and Financing

Entrepreneurial enterprises must confront significant challenges such as high-risk investments. They will also realize substantial gains in return for that risk. Banks and venture capitalists are the two principal sources of capital accumulation. Banks will have diminished oversight and reduced capital requirements from their investors. Venture capitalists incur significant capital expenditures and must meticulously oversee their investments. However, a drawback for venture investors is their expectation of substantial returns, typically ranging from 30 to 40 percent annually, which contrasts with the relatively modest yields offered by banks. The options for entrepreneurial funding include friends, family, angel investors, and seed-stage capital to facilitate initiation and operational continuity, particularly during the initial phases of the enterprise. Venture capitalists or financial organizations may provide varying levels of funding based on the initiative. Submissions may concentrate on areas such as entrepreneurship, venture capital, limited partnerships, financial venture capital funds, accounting for entrepreneurial advancement, corporate venture capital funds, small business investment firms, and institutional venture capital funds.

Track 3: Business and Organizational Management

This track continues the discussions about the challenges and prospects for advancing the “Industry 4.0” concept and the digitization of industries, companies, and industrial markets, along with an analysis of trends in the digitalization of industrial enterprise management processes. The swift advancement of disruptive technology imposes significant pressure and challenges on enterprises. Significant alterations in the operating system, business models, and organizational structure are essential to harness the advantages of contemporary technologies and maintain competitiveness. Disruptive technologies will be a prominent topic in the forthcoming years due to the rapid and profound advancements in technology and their transformative impact on industries and enterprises. Consequently, disruptive technologies that generate substantially greater value than existing ones are a persistent issue for organizational management today. The primary focus of business management is the administration and coordination of organizational activities. Management facilitates resource planning, organization, direction, and control to ensure that the business’s resources attain the objectives of the policy. The emphasis will be on business and management analytics, economic development management, e-business and e-government, digital disruptions and technology management, supply chain and logistics management, logistics, aviation and transportation in the digital era, human resource management, digital commerce and social media, design thinking and innovation management, information systems management, operations, team performance management, management education and development, and new business models.

Track 4: Entrepreneurship and Innovation

There is a growing tendency with individuals aspiring to establish their own enterprises, particularly in light of technological advancements, to focus critically on the disadvantages and challenges of utilizing new technologies and innovations. Innovation is important to contemporary entrepreneurship, resulting in numerous successful enterprises. Conversely, numerous others have encountered failures at various stages, including conceptualization, initiation, expansion, and public offers. For the majority of entrepreneurs, the perception of a vast array of options serves as the primary impetus for successful enterprises. Although entrepreneurs anticipate success, problems frequently arise first; many are dismissed at the first challenge. Success is derived from surmounting challenges; the proliferation of entrepreneurship stimulates national economic growth. The company’s capacity to expand, innovate, and digitize substantially influences its growth. At the core of any successful enterprise lies the proactive adoption of innovation and the enhancement of existing processes to attain sustainability.

Track 5: Housing and Property Markets in the Global Economy

Housing and property markets are critical in local and global economies. With Globalization influencing the local housing market, macroeconomic factors such as interest rates, inflation, foreign exchange rates, global supply chain, tariffs, and global capital flows have become critical even for simple mom-and-pop. In many regions, housing markets face dual challenges: meeting the demand for affordable housing while navigating pressures from speculative investments and regulatory policies. The rise of urbanization and population growth intensifies the need for sustainable housing solutions, while the effects of Globalization introduce opportunities for cross-border property investments and development collaborations. The topics may include but not limited to; the impact of globalization on local property markets; the role of digital transformation in property markets, housing affordability and socioeconomic equity and inclusion, real estate investments in emerging economies.

Track 6: Global Aging Society Phenomena and Alternative Asset Markets

The global aging population is reshaping economies, industries, and investment horizons, presenting both opportunities and challenges. Aging populations impact healthcare systems, labor markets, and pension schemes while influencing consumer behavior and asset allocation patterns. With traditional assets like stocks and bonds experiencing pressure from aging investors’ risk aversion, alternative assets such as real estate (REITs and MBS), private equity, infrastructure, natural resources, data centers, solar farms, and sustainable investments have become significant. These markets provide diversified opportunities for wealth accumulation, economic growth, and meeting the needs of aging societies. Key challenges include the need for innovative financial instruments to support retirement savings, investment in age-friendly infrastructure, and the alignment of alternative assets with global sustainability goals. The topics under consideration may include but not limited to; the economic impact of aging populations on global and local markets, investment trends and strategies for alternative asset markets, innovations in senior housing and age-friendly urban planning, policy responses to aging-related financial challenges.

MDM Group, South Korea

Topic: Property Developer’s Role as a Community Builder and Innovation Manager.

MDM Group is Korea’s first real estate development company to vertically integrate real estate development, finance, and asset management. MDM Group was founded in 1998 and is comprised of MDM, a developer; Korea Asset In Trust, a real estate trust company; MDM Asset Investment Management, a financial company; and the Moon Joo Scholarship Foundation.
